- Doc 1 category label: "READINESS INTELLIGENCE PLATFORM" (header) and "HōMI Technology LLC | Readiness Intelligence Platform" (footer).

### 1.3 The three-pillar scoring model
Two parallel namings of the same three dimensions appear:

| # | Doc 1 naming (Threshold Compass) | Doc 2 naming (Rings) | Ring position | Ring color (Doc 1) | Question |
|---|----------------------------------|----------------------|---------------|--------------------|----------|
| 1 | Financial Readiness | Financial Reality | **The Outer Ring** | cyan ring | "Can you afford it?" |

## 3. Target Audience & Market Positioning

### 3.1 Audience definition (Doc 2, verbatim)
- "HōMI is for anyone who's ever felt torn between what the spreadsheet says and what their gut says. For anyone who's made a decision they regretted. For anyone who wants their financial choices to feel aligned with their actual life."

### 3.2 Positioning statements
- The problem (Doc 2): "The entire financial industry profits when you say yes. **Nobody — and we mean nobody — profits from helping you be ready.** That gap is why HōMI exists."
- Doc 1 chain of conflicts: "Real estate agents make commission when you buy / Lenders profit from your mortgage / Sellers want their property sold / No one in that chain gets paid to tell you to wait."
- Doc 1, failures of current tools: "Give you data but no guidance — Zillow shows houses, doesn't tell you if you should buy / Have financial conflicts — Advisors profit when you act / Lack emotional intelligence — Spreadsheets don't know you're stressed or scared."
- Differentiation summary (Doc 2): "Financial advisors focus on affordability. Budget apps focus on tracking. Investment platforms focus on returns. None of them ask: 'Are you emotionally ready?' or 'Is the timing actually right?' We ask all three. And we have no financial incentive to push you toward yes."

### 3.3 Competitive comparison table (Doc 2, verbatim content)
| Category | What It Does | What It Misses |
|---|---|---|
| Budget Apps | Track where money goes | Doesn't help you decide what to do with it |
| Financial Advisors | Give advice on finances | Can't measure emotional readiness or timing |
| Investment Platforms | Help you invest | Don't tell you if you're ready to invest |
| Mortgage/Auto Lenders | Approve loans | Have incentive for you to say yes, not be ready |
| HōMI | Measures financial, emotional, and temporal readiness. Profits only when you're clear. | — |

### 3.4 Business model / alignment claims (Doc 2, verbatim)
- "HōMI profits only when you're ready."
- "We don't take a commission on transactions / We don't get paid by lenders or sellers / We don't profit if you buy something / **We profit directly: You pay for clarity**"

### 3.5 Expansion roadmap (Doc 1)
Home buying first, then: "Buying a car / Switching careers / Starting a business / Getting married / Having kids / Retiring early."

## 4. Voice & Messaging Guidelines

> **Gap:** No explicit style guide (tone descriptor lists, do/don't word tables) exists in these documents. The following is inferred from consistent usage patterns — labeled as **inference**, not stated rules.

Observed voice characteristics (inference, with evidence):
- **Blunt, anti-sales, plain-spoken:** "making a $400,000 decision based on gut feeling, pressure from a salesperson, or FOMO from friends is insane." (Doc 1)
- **Empathetic-but-honest "friend" persona:** "I love you, but you're not ready yet." (Doc 1)
- **Analogy-driven:** "You wouldn't let your buddy perform surgery on you just because he's enthusiastic — why would you trust someone with a commission on the line…" (Doc 1); founder's spine-surgery analogy (Doc 2).
- **Repetition-for-emphasis device:** "truly align", "Nobody — and we mean nobody", "Not just able. Ready."
- **Triadic rhetorical structure** mirrors the three rings: "your head, your heart, and your timing"; "The math. / The feeling. / The window."
- **Distinguishes "able" vs "ready"** as a core lexical pair: "People will stop buying things they're not ready for. They'll stop waiting on decisions they're ready to make."
- **Negation-first framing** to define category: "We're not a budget app. We're not therapy. We're not a financial advisor."

Founder note (Doc 2, attributed "**— Cody Short, Founder**"): "I built HōMI because I've seen what happens when people mistake momentum for readiness… I've had six back surgeries. Every time, I learned the same lesson: you can't rush alignment. The spine either heals or it doesn't. Forcing it breaks you worse… We help you find your threshold — the moment when all three rings align and you're actually ready. Not just able. Ready."

Mission statement (Doc 2, verbatim): "**Our Mission: We imagine a world where readiness precedes action. Where decisions are timed with clarity, not pressure. Where people stop confusing affordability with readiness. Where your yes finally belongs to you.**"
